Numeric thresholds when to bet, and how cautiously

Every threshold has a line below it explaining what happens if you raise or lower it. You don't need to touch them: the starting values are fine to begin with.

The bot only looks at markets that resolve within this many days. Lower = money comes back sooner and you get quick feedback on how well the bot does, but fewer markets to choose from. Higher = more choice, but the money stays committed longer and results take months to arrive.

How far the bot must diverge from the market price before the prediction counts as a real bet. Higher = it only bets when it strongly disagrees with the market, so it bets less often. Lower = it bets even on small disagreements, so it bets more often.

How informed the bot must declare itself before the "cautious" rule lets it bet. Higher = it holds back more often, only betting when it says it is very informed. Lower = it bets even when it declares low confidence.

Below how many dollars of trading a market gets ignored entirely. Higher = it only looks at large, liquid markets. Lower = it also looks at small, thinly traded markets.

How much of the virtual bankroll to risk on each single bet, following a standard risk-management formula. Higher = bigger bets, more pronounced wins and losses. Lower = smaller, more cautious bets.

What the bot sees which information it receives before answering

The market price never enters this information: otherwise we would be measuring whether the bot can read a number, not whether it can judge a situation.

Who makes the decision which engine answers, or how many together

Three engines can answer the exact same way to the same questions: Jev (remote, free but with a call quota), Laya and Kev (both on your own machine, no call limit but slower). You can use just one, or have them vote together.

Whoever has the highest weight wins when the three engines disagree. With the starting values, Jev wins on its own even if both Laya and Kev say the opposite, because its weight outweighs their sum.

The local engines (Laya, Kev) must be started separately on your machine. If they are not running, the bot will only find out when it tries to get an answer from them: see platform/ENGINES.md for how to start them.
