What a bot is, in here

A bot is a set of rules. It decides three things:

  • Which markets to watch on Polymarket (all of them, or just one category such as geopolitics).
  • How much context to give Jev before asking it for a probability: the market's rules, the dates, recent news.
  • How much disagreement with the market price is needed before the bot decides to bet, instead of standing aside.
No real money ever moves. Every bot has a fake bankroll, it only serves to size simulated bets, to see whether the bot's reasoning holds up over time, not to make money.
Jev is not a chatbot. It doesn't converse and has no memory of the chat. It's a function: it receives the state of a market (rules, dates, news) and returns a typed number, a probability between 0 and 1. Everything that happens after that, whether to bet or not, how much to bet, is decided by the bot's rules, not by Jev.

Give your bot a name

The name is only used to recognize it in the list. You can create more later, with different names and rules.

How cautious should it be

Pick a starting style. These are numeric thresholds already set, you don't need to fully understand them to get started.

Want to tune the numeric thresholds yourself (minimum edge, minimum evidence, volume, Kelly fraction)? You can do that later, any time, from the bot's Settings.

Done

  1. Press Run it on the bot's page: it will look at markets and evaluate whether to bet, following the rules you just picked.
  2. Predictions show up right away, but they don't prove anything yet. It takes quite a few before you can say whether the bot gets it right.
  3. Real results arrive once Polymarket resolves the markets: that can take anywhere from a few days to a few weeks.
